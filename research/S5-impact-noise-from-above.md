# S5. Impact noise from the unit above: estimate it or keep it out of scope?

Research for issue #37, task S5. Feeds the limit stated for building sources in section 5 of
`docs/UNIT_PROFILE_PLAN.md` (branch `codex/unit-profile-plan`). US housing. No model code.
Every value has a URL and an exact quote, or is marked `unsourced`.

## Answer

**Keep impact noise out of scope for the Quiet Score.** Show the floor's impact rating class as
a label instead, and say plainly that footsteps and drops are not in the number.

Reasons, all from the sources below:

1. **The ratings do not describe footsteps.** IIC, AIIC and NISR (ASTM E989) are defined only
   for the standard tapping machine. E989 itself says the rating "strictly only applies to
   excitation by the standard tapping machine" and "does not deal with low frequency sounds
   below 100 Hz that are typically generated below lightweight joist floors when they are
   walked on". Most US apartments in Houston and Los Angeles are wood frame (see S6), which is
   exactly that case.
2. **A tapping-machine rating does not predict a walker.** In NRC's 75-floor study (Warnock,
   NRCC-34003), IIC from the tapping machine against IIC from a real walker gave
   r^2 = 0.37, and A-weighted level against walker A-weighted level gave r^2 = 0.07. Carpet
   raised a concrete slab's IIC by 61 points while walker levels at 50 Hz fell by only 8 dB.
3. **The source is not standardized.** The same study says "There is too much variation
   between walkers and no practical hope of standardization of the footstep impact". The model
   would need a footstep source level in dBA per step and an activity profile; neither exists
   in any source found.
4. **The ISO 12354-2 prediction route needs inputs we do not have** (lab Ln,w of the bare
   floor, the covering's improvement, flanking data per junction), was built for heavy
   homogeneous floors, and still predicts only a tapping-machine level. The COST FP0702 group
   says the EN 12354 model needs "refining and adjusting" before it fits lightweight buildings.
5. **Gym weight drops** are a third, separate source (heavy-hard impact). ASTM's method for it
   is still a work item, not a standard.

What the model can say, from floor construction alone, is a **rank and a class**: bare
concrete with hard finish is poor (IIC about 25), concrete with carpet and pad is very good
(IIC about 86 in the lab), a typical wood joist floor with resilient ceiling is about IIC 45,
and a joist floor with ceiling fastened direct to joists is about IIC 32. Wood joist floors can
still produce "thumping" complaints above IIC 50. That class can be shown next to the score as
"impact rating: likely poor / fair / good, not included in the score".

## Equations

### E1. ISO 12354-2 simplified model (shown to make the input gap visible, not to adopt)

```
L'n,w   = Ln,w,eq - dLw + K                # field impact level, tapping machine
Ln,w,eq ≈ 164 - 35*log10(m')               # bare homogeneous floor, m' in kg/m2, 100..600
IIC     ≈ 110 - Ln,w                       # sign change between ISO and ASTM ratings
```

- `Ln,w,eq`: equivalent weighted normalized impact level of the bare structural floor
- `dLw`: improvement from the covering, which must be measured on the same base floor
- `K`: flanking correction, which depends on the mass of the flanking walls (value table in
  the standard, not read here: `unsourced`)

Worked example: a 150 mm concrete slab at about 2400 kg/m3 has m' = 360 kg/m2 (density
`unsourced`, a common textbook value). Ln,w,eq = 164 - 35*log10(360) = 164 - 35*2.556 = 74.5 dB,
so IIC is about 110 - 74.5 = 35.5. NRC measured IIC 25 for a bare 150 mm slab. The 10-point
gap is the kind of error this route carries even for the floor type it was built for. For a
wood joist floor E1 does not apply at all (not homogeneous, m' far below 100 kg/m2).

### E2. Why a tapping-machine level cannot become a footstep dBA

The NRC r^2 of 0.07 between tapping-machine dBA and walker dBA means the tapping machine
explains about 7% of the variance in walker level across floors. A weighted tapping spectrum
(the measured tapping-machine bands plus +15, +15, +10, +5 dB at 50, 63, 80, 100 Hz) raised r^2 to about 0.8 in one lab,
but that gives a rating that ranks floors, not a dBA level for a footstep, because no footstep
source level is standardized.

## Conflicts

1. **IIC as a proxy for annoyance.** Builders and codes treat IIC 50 as the target (CTU 35);
   E989, E3207 and NRC all say IIC misses the low-frequency thump of wood floors. **Take both:**
   IIC ranks hard-surfaced concrete correctly, and fails for wood joist floors.
2. **Carpet.** On concrete, carpet raises IIC from 25 to 86 and occupants are content. On wood
   joists, the same carpet changes little below 100 Hz. **Never credit carpet on a wood floor
   with an impact improvement in the UI.**
3. **Bodlund versus Warnock.** Warnock needed +5 to +15 dB extra weight at 50 to 100 Hz to
   match walkers; Bodlund (cited by Warnock) did not. Warnock suggests his lab floors' 50 Hz
   peak as the reason. Unresolved; another reason not to build a number on it.
4. **ISO 12354-2 versus measurement.** E1 gives about IIC 35 for a 150 mm slab, NRC measured
   25. Not used, so no choice needed.
